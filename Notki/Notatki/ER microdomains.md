# ER morphological sections
The basic (traditional) classification of different ER domains is:
- ER sheets / rough ER (heavy) : responsible for the protein translation and secretion
- ER tubules / smooth ER (light): responsible for the lipid biosynthesis and detoxification 
But it is simplified view: 3D structure is more fine tuned, and there are ribosomes on ER tubules
[@Obara2023StructuralDiversityEndoplasmicReticulumMicroscale]


More recent studies using super resolution microscopy defined more structures in the ER (from nucleus to the outside) [@Obara2023StructuralDiversityEndoplasmicReticulumMicroscale]:
- Nuclear envelope: single, porous ER sheet (two bilayers with lumen in between) separating nucleus from the cytosol. A story of its own. Connected to the ER through NE-ER junctions: tubule-like filaments
- Stacked sheets: in widefield they appear as blurs, but in superresolution they are stacked with uniform spacing (CLIMP63, p180 and KTN proteins responsible for spacing; Reticulons and DP1/REEP/YOP1P for curvature at the tips). Prevalent in secretory cells: probably location of secretory proteins translation. The sheet like structures are likely stabilized by polyribosomes attached to the membrane via translocon complex and stabilized by the RNA molecule (not tested well though)
- ER cisternae: few large cisterns of ER, (IMHO collapsed or not resolved sheets). Likely used for translation
- Three way junctions and ER matrices: ER tubules often connect and merge with each other (Atlastin1 and Lunapark proteins) making three-way junctions, which likely relieve the tension on the lipid bilayer. Many such junction, cross-connected form matrix. Matrices have high surface area to volume ratio, so they are thought to represent sites of trans-membrane protein translation and storage
- ER tubules: the distal part of ER is composed mostly of single tubules extending all the way to the cytoplasmic membrane. They contain less ribosomes, than sheets, but still have some. In neurons can be very long (IMHO related with the local translation)

ER tubules can be extended (quickly) through active transport along microtubules or hitchhiking when connected to other nuclear structures (ex. lysosomes). Also pTAC and dTAC when microtubule itself is etended or disbanded [@Obara2023StructuralDiversityEndoplasmicReticulumMicroscale]

Very understudied is the function of lipids and differences in lipid composition on the ER structure and function [@Obara2023StructuralDiversityEndoplasmicReticulumMicroscale]

# ERES (ER Exit Sites)
Sites of vesicle formation, which then merges with the Golgi apparatus. Crucial for secretory proteins. Doesn't have ribosomes, so should be translationally inactive. Maintained by COPI and COPII proteins also TANGO1 and Sec16A [@Farhan2025UnifiedFrameworkFunctionEndoplasmicReticulum]

**ERGIC** ER-Golgi intermediate compartment (connects ERES with Golgi)
