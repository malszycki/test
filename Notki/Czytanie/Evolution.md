- In 90s there was a lot of studies on *Giardia intestinalis*, which was suspected not to have mitochondria. It was later proven wrong: these amitochondrial protists lost their mitochondria due to reductive evolution [@Tovar2003MitochondrialRemnantOrganellesGiardiaFunction]


- whole genome duplication can give rise to speciation and increased evolutionary rates due to reducing genotypic constraints. Duplicated genes can mutate to acquire new functions (neofunctionalization) or more specialized functions (sub-functionalization) [@Falcon2023TransposonWavesWatertolandTransition]
	- WGD de-repression also seems to provide an opprotunity for transposon expansion. Maybe also due to bottlenecking during re-diploidization.
	- WGD doesn't lead to dramatic increase in gene numbers, as all the vertebrates have similar number of genes. 

- There are examples of ultraconserved regulatory sequences derived from transposons [@Falcon2023TransposonWavesWatertolandTransition]
- KRAB zinc finger proteins may have driven transposon expansion in vertebrates (KRABs evolved in ancestor of tetrapods, just before the expansion), also KRAB number correlates with genome size [@Falcon2023TransposonWavesWatertolandTransition]
