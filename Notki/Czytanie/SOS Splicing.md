[@Zhao2026RNASplicingSystemThatExcises]
- observed previously in *C. elegans* in 1996 in functional assay (transposon insertion didn't ablate gene function), but then explained by simple splicing
- Primarily excised [[DNA transposons] from exonic RNA.
- much less precise, than [[Splicing|spliceosome]]: Tc1: 78% in frame Tc3: most not in frame, GU-AG are the most common splice sites, but only about 20%, all other donor-acceptor combinations are also used. Generates a lot of indels
- Does not require functional splicing (PlaB tested)
- Most likely mechanism is recognition by AKAP17 ([[Nuclear speckles]] protein in humans) and exon ligation through RTCB (through CAAP1 recruitment to AKAP17) ligase
- "*The data suggest that, in human cells, CAAP1 recruits RTCB to nuclear speckles, where it interacts with AKAP17A to promote SOS splicing by ligating the mRNA fragments generated during the TE-excision step of SOS splicing*"
- Mechanism of TE excision is unknown. Tested bunch of endonucleases, but none of them were required.

- IDEA for experiment: Target AKAP17 to specific RNA (Cas something fusion?) to see if you can force SOS splicing into some RNA.
