Many mRNAs are localized and translated at the site of protein function to make the transport more efficient: c-myc and methalothionein localize close to nucleus, and are transported inside, ATM1 close to mitochondria and rice prolamines near ER prolamine bodies [@Jansen2001MRNALocalizationMessageMove].

There are three non-exclusive hypothesized (and somewhat observed) models of RNA localization [@Jansen2001MRNALocalizationMessageMove]:
- Active transport: cis-element on RNA is bound by RBP, which then associates with cytoskeleton motor proteins, gets transported and anchors at the site of localization to prevent its diffusion. Both nuclear and cytoplasmic RBPs coordinate this transport
- Local transcript stabilization: transcripts are locally protected from decay, which leads to their local accumulation. Observed for Hsp83 transcripts in Drosophila embryos, but also SON in Drosophila was shown to form droplets, which locally protect some RNA from decay `TENT5 locally stabilizes transcripts: maybe that is the mechanism of localization as well?`
- Facilitated diffusion: RNAs diffuse freely and accumulate at the site due to their anchoring. May be translation dependent (like in ER targeted translation `citation needed`), or may be anchoring through cytoskeleton-bound RBP /ncRNA (ex. Staufen protein and bcd RNA in Drosophila [@Jansen2001MRNALocalizationMessageMove])
