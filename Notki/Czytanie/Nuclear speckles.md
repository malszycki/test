# Speckle microphases
- Oded Regev proposed a model, where SR-coated exons partition inside a speckle and hnRNP-coated introns partition outside. This would position the splice-sites at the interphase between speckles and outside and allow the splicing to recognize splice sites and splice exons together (excluding introns) [@Liao2021SplicingPhaseseparatedNuclearSpeckleInterface]
- This model could be expanded with the recent Rohit Pappu's finding where SRSF proteins create their own microphases within the nuclear speckles, which would immensely increase the interface surface [@Shinn2026NuclearSpeckleProteinsFormIntrinsic]
- It could explain the mechanism of post-transcriptional exon-intron recognition quite well

# Speckles do co-transcriptional or post-transcriptional splicing?

- the most recent study using minigenes showed increased **co-transcriptional** splicing efficiency of a minigene near speckles independent of the exon-intron architecture [@Bhat2024GenomeOrganizationNuclearSpecklesDrives]

- many studies (especially older ones) associate speckles with post-transcriptional splicing using different methodologies [@Girard2012PosttranscriptionalSpliceosomesAreRetainedNuclear; @Wen2025RNALocalizationNuclearSpecklesFollows] `need more citations`

- The more I read the more I think, that the speckle-regulated exon-introns may be post-transcriptionally spliced (maybe via microphases)
